Privacy policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026.

Vinyl for Apple TV (“the app”) is designed to do as little with your data as possible. It is a viewer for a Discogs collection you already own — nothing more. This policy describes, in plain language, what the app accesses, what it stores, and what it never does.

01

Who runs the app

The app is built and maintained by Andrei Sudarikov as an independent project. There is no company, no server, and no third-party operator behind it. Source code is publicly visible at github.com/andreisudarikov19/vinyl-art-apple-tv.

02

What the app accesses

  • Your Discogs account.When you sign in, the app uses Discogs’s OAuth flow so you can authorise it to read your collection. The app receives an access token (the key the app uses to reach Discogs), which it uses to read your Discogs username, list the records you own and read their public metadata (artist, title, tracklist, year, catalogue number). The app only reads: it never makes changes to your Discogs account.
  • Cover-art lookups.For every record in your collection, the app queries the public MusicBrainz API by artist, title and, if the record has one, catalogue number. It uses that to find the matching release, then asks the public Cover Art Archive for its front cover. When the Cover Art Archive has one, the app shows it in place of the Discogs cover. These are anonymous queries — no account information is sent.
03

What the app stores

  • Stored on your Apple TV, and what leaves it.Your Discogs collection is saved on the device. Cover images are saved on the device too. The system may also clear your saved collection and cover images when space is low. Your access token (the key the app uses to reach Discogs) stays in the Apple TV’s secure storage, on this device only. It is never copied to other devices through iCloud, Apple’s online storage service. Your collection stays on the Apple TV; only the release details for cover lookups leave it.
  • Sent online to find cover art.For every record, the app searches MusicBrainz, an online music database, for the matching release. It then asks the Cover Art Archive, an online image library, for its front cover.
  • Display choices.Your layout, sort order and display settings are saved on the Apple TV with the app’s other data.
  • Not in iCloud.The app stores nothing in iCloud, Apple’s online storage service.
04

What the app does not do

  • No analytics.The app does not include Google Analytics, Firebase, Mixpanel, Segment, Sentry, Crashlytics, or any other telemetry SDK. Crash logs that you choose to share with Apple via the system-level Share with App Developers setting are the only signal the developer ever receives, and they contain no personal information beyond what Apple itself anonymises.
  • No advertising.The app shows no ads and contains no advertising SDKs. There is no tracker that follows you between apps or websites.
  • No third-party servers.The app talks directly to Discogs, Cover Art Archive, and MusicBrainz over HTTPS. The Cover Art Archive is a joint project of MusicBrainz and the Internet Archive, so its images download from the Internet Archive’s servers. There is no backend operated by the developer.
  • No data sale.The developer never sells, shares, or transmits any personal information about you, because the developer never receives any to begin with.

07

Your choices

  • Signing out: before handing over or switching accounts.To hand the Apple TV to someone else, or to sign in with a different Discogs account, choose Sign out & erase library first. If you keep the library instead, it is erased when the app next builds the library for a different Discogs account on this Apple TV.
  • Signing out: keep library.Choose Sign out (keep library) only if you plan to sign back in to the same account later. It removes the Discogs sign-in and keeps your library. A kept library belongs only to the Discogs account it came from. To delete it later, sign in to that account and choose Sign out & erase library.
  • Signing out: erase library.To remove your saved collection from the Apple TV, choose Sign out & erase library. It happens straight away, with no confirmation step. It removes the Discogs sign-in and your saved collection. Your collection on Discogs is not changed. The cover images are kept after you erase the library. Deleting the app removes them.
  • Signing out: display choices.Your layout, sort order and display settings stay after either sign-out option, so the next person sees them too. To clear them as well, delete the app, as described under Deleting data. Both of the sign-out options are in the Settings menu, opened from the gear icon in the gallery toolbar.
  • Revoking access.You can disconnect the app from your Discogs account at any time from your Discogs application settings. Doing so immediately invalidates the access token. The next time the app reaches Discogs (it does each time it starts, and when you open a record), it returns to the welcome screen and asks you to sign in again. Your library stays on the Apple TV, so signing back in to the same account shows it at once.
  • Deleting data.To free space, delete the app. Erasing the library keeps the cover images. Deleting the app from your Apple TV removes your saved collection and the cover images. Deleting the app also removes your layout, sort order and display settings. They are stored with the app’s data. The app does not copy the token to other devices through iCloud. The app keeps the token when you delete it, and removes the token the first time it runs after you install the app again. You then sign in afresh. To remove the token straight away, choose Sign out & erase library in the Settings menu before deleting the app. You can also revoke access from your Discogs application settings at any time.
